# IIA — 90-Day Go-to-Market Plan

**Primary objective:** win paying AI-service customers quickly, then standardize the service into a repeatable IIA product.

**Guardrail:** work on a feature branch and validate locally/staging first. Do not change production deployment, DNS, secrets, or the existing live website without a separate explicit authorization.

## First offer

Sell an IIA Business Knowledge Assistant that answers a narrow set of questions from client-approved FAQs and documents, with a clear fallback/human handoff. Avoid selling a generic “AI for everything” promise.

## Days 1–14: validate and prepare

- Select one initial niche (training institutes are a reasonable first test; compare with consultancies and service businesses).
- Interview 5 potential buyers about repeated questions, current handling time, source material, and budget.
- Build one demo using synthetic or authorized sample content.
- Prepare a one-page service offer, quote template, pilot scope, privacy/data handling summary, and demo script.
- Run the production-readiness checklist before handling real client data.
- Build a prospect list of 20 businesses and send personalized outreach.

**Exit criteria:** demo can be shown reliably; 5 buyer conversations completed; 20 prospects researched; at least 2 discovery/demo meetings requested. These are targets, not guarantees.

## Days 15–30: sell one scoped pilot

- Contact 5 qualified prospects per working day, personalized and manually reviewed.
- Offer a 15-minute discovery call and a short demo.
- Quote a paid pilot at NPR 8,000–12,000 with clear limits.
- Take a written scope and payment agreement before implementation.
- Test against agreed questions; document failures and fixes.
- Ask for feedback and permission before publishing any case study.

**Exit criteria:** target one paid pilot. If no buyer is willing to pay after 20–30 qualified contacts and several conversations, interview prospects and change the niche/problem/offer before adding features.

## Days 31–60: convert delivery into a service

- Complete the pilot against the written acceptance criteria.
- Offer a managed package at NPR 15,000–25,000 setup plus NPR 3,000–6,000/month.
- Track setup hours, support hours, model/API costs, hosting, and gross contribution.
- Turn repeated setup steps into a checklist and reusable configuration.
- Seek two or three additional clients through the first demo and referrals.

**Exit criteria:** one repeatable onboarding procedure, measured per-client costs, and a renewal offer.

## Days 61–90: prove repeatability

- Review which niche converts and which support burden is manageable.
- Improve onboarding, usage limits, source references, fallback behavior, monitoring, and backup/restore.
- Publish one factual case study only with client approval.
- Test partner referrals only after the paid offer and delivery process are stable.
- Consider self-service subscriptions/API only if customers ask for them and usage costs can be metered.

**Exit criteria:** at least one measurable customer outcome, a defined delivery process, known contribution margins, and a decision backed by evidence about the next niche/product.

## Weekly scorecard

Record:
- Prospects researched and contacted.
- Replies, discovery calls, demos, proposals, paid pilots, and lost reasons.
- Setup fee collected and monthly recurring revenue (MRR).
- Model/API spend, hosting, support time, refunds, and unpaid invoices.
- Answer quality on agreed test questions.
- Customer satisfaction and renewal/referral intent.

Do not interpret outreach activity as revenue. Update pricing based on real willingness to pay and delivery cost.
